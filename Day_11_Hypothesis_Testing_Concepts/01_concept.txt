-------------------
Hypothesis Testing:
-------------------

Statistical method to decide whether the results we are seeing is real or just a random chance.

-------------------------
1. Null Hypothesis (H₀):
-------------------------

- Statement that nothing has change/ no effect/ no difference.
- Examples: 
    > H₀: The average rating before and after an update is the same.
    > H₀: Men and women have equal average salaries.
    > H₀: A coin is fair (p = 0.5).

-------------------------------------
2. Alternative Hypothesis (H₁ / Ha):
-------------------------------------

- Statement that we want to test/prove.
- Examples:
    > Ha: The average rating has changed.
    > Ha: Men and women have different salaries.
    > Ha: The coin is not fair (p ≠ 0.5).

----------------------------------
3. Type I Error (False Positive):
----------------------------------
- Rejecting H₀, even though it is true.
- Think of it as "YOU THOUGHT THERE WAS AN EFFECT, BUT ACTUALLY THERE WASN'T".
- Example: A pregnancy test shows positive even though the person is not pregnant. 
            (You falsely detected something that doesn't exist).

----------------------------------
4. Type II Error (False Negative):
----------------------------------
- Fail to reject H₀ even though it is false.
- Think of it as "YOU MISSED THE EFFECT EVEN THOUGH IT WAS REAL".
- Example: A pregnancy test shows negative even though the person is pregnant.
            (You failed to detect something that actually exists).

------------------------------------------------------
|* Super Easy Memory Trick 🔥                        |
|    Type I → I magine something that’s not there     |
|    Type II → You’re T oo blind to see what’s there  |   
-------------------------------------------------------

---------------------------
5. Significance Level (α):
---------------------------

- Threshold to reject H₀.
- Common Values: 0.05, 0.01
- Meaning of α: 0.05 -> Accepting a 5% chance of being wrong when rejecting H₀

------------
6. p-value:
------------

- How likely the observed result is, assuming H₀ is true.
    - Small p-value (<α): result is too likely under H₀ (reject H₀)
    - Large p-value (>α): result is plausible under H₀ (failed to reject H₀)

- Examples:
    p = 0.02 with α = 0.05 → reject H₀
    p = 0.21 → fail to reject H₀

---------------------------------------
7. Basic Hypothesis Testing Procedure:
---------------------------------------

- State H₀ and Ha
- Choose significance level α
- Compute test statistic from data
- Compute p-value
- Compare p-value with α → conclude
